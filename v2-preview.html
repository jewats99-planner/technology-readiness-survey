Technology Investment Reality Check

Before investing in new technology, examine the questions your organization should be able to answer.

This is not a quick survey and it is not intended to give your organization a superficial readiness score. It is a structured assessment designed to help your team identify business, process, information, organizational and investment issues that may affect whether a technology initiative creates the value you expect.

Take your time. Discuss questions where appropriate. If a question exposes uncertainty, disagreement or an assumption you cannot support, that may be one of the most valuable results of the assessment.

About the “select team.” Throughout this assessment, select team means the people chosen to help define and evaluate the proposed change. They should not be selected simply by title or seniority, but because collectively they understand the affected work, can identify problems candidly, can distinguish requirements from preferences, and can contribute thoughtful solutions. No single person should be assumed to possess all of the knowledge the decision requires.

Your Decision Roadmap

This is not a readiness score. The purpose is to identify what appears established and what deserves further investigation before the next significant commitment.

Executive Test

If you had to recommend this investment tomorrow, could you explain: What problem are we solving? What evidence demonstrates that it matters? Why does the organization work the way it does today? Which current practices are true requirements and which may be changed? What requirements do customers and other outside parties impose? What information and relationships will the future require? Have we considered capabilities we may not have known were possible? Do we have the right people, knowledge and capacity? What assumptions underlie the expected benefits? What will the complete investment actually require? What is the smallest meaningful next step? And will that step leave us room to learn and grow?

If you cannot confidently answer all of those questions, that does not necessarily mean you should stop the initiative. It means the assessment has identified what you should investigate next.
